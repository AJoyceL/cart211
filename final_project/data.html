What Is Surveillance Capitalism?

Zuboff defines surveillance capitalism as a new economic logic in which companies (firms):

  • Claim human experience as free, raw material: every click, movement, message, pause, gesture, and search becomes data.
  • Translate this data into predictions about behaviour: companies use machine learning to turn behavioural data into “prediction products”.
  • Sell these predictions to advertisers or clients: the goal is to predict –and eventually influence– what people will do next.
  • Operate through hidden extraction:

    • It depends on :

    • Secret data collection
    • Opaque algorithms
    • Asymmetry between what companies know and what users know.

Behavioural Surplus

Not all collected data is needed to serve user. Companies extract behavioural surplus –extra data that goes beyond service improvement.

  • Google
    • Interests
    • Habits
    • Intentions
  • Amazon
    • What users search but don’t buy
    • Over patterns on product pages
    • Timing of purchases, refund behaviour
    • Smart speaker(Alexa) voice patterns
  • Apple Health(Wearables)
    • Heart rate variation
    • Sleep rhythm
    • Movement patterns throughout the day
    • Location + activity combinations

Prediction Markets

Surveillance capitalism created new markets based on traded predictions of human behaviour, such as Google and Facebook, who compete to gather the most precise predictive data to sell more accurate behaviour forecasts.

Data Analysis and Instrumentarian Power

Zuboff introduces the term instrumentarian power as a new kind of influence that does not need to persuade or control through ideology. It, instead:

  • Monitors behaviour continuously
  • Nudges users through personalised cues
  • Shifts actions subtly without conscious awareness.

It becomes a form of behavioural governance

Extraction Architecture

Surveillance capitalism depends on systems designed to extract data everywhere, all the time. This architecture makes behaviour observable, measurable and modifiable.

  • Smartphone
  • Smart home devices
  • Wearables
  • Location tracking
  • Social media
  • Internet/search webs

Problem: Loss of Autonomy and Future Control

This system threatens:

  • Individual autonomy
  • Democratic institutions
  • The right to a private future

Because behaviour is continuously monitored and shape, people lose:

  • The ability to act freely
  • Control over their own choices
  • The boundary between public and private life.

Surveillance capitalism turns human experience into raw material or data extraction, uses advanced analysis to predict and influence behaviour, and creates economic incentives to monitor and shape human action in wats that undermine autonomy and democracy.
