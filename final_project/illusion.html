The illusion of choice

Do you get it now? We've all been made into numbers, statistics and predictions for those who have the means to buy them. Everything you see, feel or listen to is carefully curated to fit your person, but then, if that is the case, have we ever had a choice? Or is it all but a beautiful illusion?

Zuboff, in some part, argues that surveillance capitalism creates an illusion of independence and choice online while quietly predetermining outcomes that suits the analysis of your behavioural data.

You feel independent because you see choices –but they’re all engineered

These firms - platforms such as Facebook, Google, Instagram, and more recently, TikTok - present us with menus, recommendations, options to click, videos to watch, routes to take, people to follow. It gives the concept of choice, that all of it feels like your decision, but they’re curated through algorithms designed to produce specific outcomes: more engagement, more data, more predictability of your future behaviour from the collection and analysis of your data fed through the algorithm.

You choose, but only from a set of options place in front of you for you.

Predictions become prescriptions

The model given to us by Zuboff is a cycle of behavioural value reinvestment: the observation of your behaviour to analyse and predict your behavioural data, and to ultimately shape your experience.

This is the shift from prediction markets to behavioural modification markets. This system doesn’t simply behave in a certain way for advertisers, it nudges, influences and arranges your environment to make is engineered outcome more ultimately likely.

In a way, it feels like you’re choosing, but that path towards it was already paved.

“Freedom of choice” becomes a managed experience

Your online freedom is largely a performance:

  • You think you’re choosing a product, but the search results are ranked to favour paid outcomes or higher-profit predictions. (Amazon, Shein, Google, Steam, etc)
  • You think you’re discovering a video, but the recommendation engine knowns what will keep you scrolling longest. (Youtube, Tik Tok, Instagram, Facebook, etc)
  • You think you’re searching on your own, but the autocomplete steers your query, shaping what you consider in the first place.

It isn’t coercion, it is an architected space in which your choices are crafted.

Independence is preserved on the surface so manipulation can deepen underneath

For surveillance capitalism to work, it must allow you to feel free. If people feel controlled or watched, they would resist, and so, the solution was for the system to hide its influence by:

  • Making the interfaces look neutral.
  • Making choices feel individualised.
  • Making recommendations framed as helpful.
  • Making invisible nudges.

This what Zuboff calls the legitimising effect of personalisation: masking the fact that the system’s real objective is not your autonomy, but your predictable behaviour.

The outcome is predetermined because the system has incentives to shape it

The outcome is predetermined because the system has incentives to shape it

  • Design environments that funnel you towards predictable reactions.
  • Strategically arranges information to maximise certain behaviours.
  • Test and optimise ways to influence your choices.

This means the system benefits less from your independence and more from your predictability. So independence is allowed – encouraged, even – as long as it doesn't disrupt the predicted outcome.

Put simply, you feel free because the system profits and needs to feel free. It gives you the ability to act freely within a space designed to make your”free” choices profitable, and often, the decision you make has already been shaped lon before you click.
