Behavioural Surplus: the Raw Material

Platforms collect far more data than they notify, or need to perform their basic service:

  • Scroll speed
  • What you pause on
  • Voice inflections picked up by smart speakers
  • Location trails
  • Patterns of swiping, skipping, liking and hesitating

It’s valuable because it reveals”

  • Preferences
  • Habits
  • Intentions
  • Vulnerabilities
  • Future behaviour

Algorithms Transform This surplus into prediction

Once the surplus is collected, machine-learning algorithms process it.

They detect patterns: they find correlations that humans cannot see, tiny signals that reveal what a person will likely do.

They build behavioural models:

  • What you will click
  • What you will buy
  • How you will react
  • When you are most vulnerable or receptive

They improve over time: algorithms get more accurate the more behavioural surplus they consume, creating a feedback loop.

More data -> better predictions -> more profitable -> incentive to collect even more data.

How algorithms profit: prediction products

Algorithms turn behavioural surplus into prediction products.

What is prediction products?

  • A bundle on insights that forecast what you or a group like you will do and how you can be nudged.

These prediction products are sold in behaviour future markets:

  • Advertisers
  • Insurers
  • Political campaigns
  • Many other buyers

This is where the profit occurs.

How it makes money: microtargetting + influence

The goal isn’t only to predict your future behaviour, but to shape it by influencing your behaviour through:

  • Personalised ads
  • Tailored content
  • Specific timing
  • Emotional triggers
  • Manipulation of interface to guide actions

The more behaviour the system can influence, the more valuable the prediction market becomes.

The algorithmic feedback loop

  • Extract behaviour surplus.
  • Analyse it
  • Predict future behaviour
  • Sell these predictions
  • Nudge users to behave predictably
  • Extract even more data from those nudged behaviour
  • Refine predictions => more profit

This creates an ever-expanding cycle of behavioural extraction and control

Why is this so powerful?

The want for variability to disappear: the more predictable you are, the more profitable you are.

Shaping environments to guide your next more: feeds, recommendations, notifications, targeted ads designed tor educe uncertainty in your future behaviour.

Creating ”instrumentarian power”: a system that modifies behaviour remotely and continuously without needing your awareness or consent.

Summary

It feeds on behavioural surplus by:

  • Collection excessive behavioural data
  • Turning it into behavioural predictions
  • Selling these predictions in markets
  • Modifying tour behaviour to make predictions more profitable
  • Using your future behaviour as a steady stream of income.

Your behaviour becomes a commodity.
