What Is Data?

Data, in it’s simplest term, is defined as a value or a set of values representing a specific concept or concepts - information in the form of facts and statistics - that are collected for reference or analysis. It, however, is but “information” until it is analysed and possibly combined with others in order to extract meaning or/and to provide context; then, and only then, does it become data. While seemingly simple, the meaning can vary depending on the context. It includes, but isn’t limited to, means of communication or representation of knowledge such as number, text, images, measurements, observations or facts about people, objects, events, or systems.

Source: ressources.data.gov

What Is Data? pt2

Data, in philosophy, refers as things known or assumed as facts. It makes up the basic elements from which knowledge is built. It gives people the ability to infer and predict behaviour or data from observation.

It is exactly “that” that makes up behavioural data, a by-product of surveillance capitalism, collected from observing online engagement and interaction, consisting of materials such as online actions and activities, purchases, account and system interactions, marketing and communication, customer service and feedback, negative actions and preferences. It is a lucrative by-product used, stored and/or sold by firms.
